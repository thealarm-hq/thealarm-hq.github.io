Terms of Use

Last updated: November 11, 2026

In short

2. What The Alarm offers

(1) The Alarm is an alarm app that works like a calendar: alarms for any day, with rules, exceptions, public holidays, vacations and shift rhythms – plus a world clock, stopwatch and timers. The app and its App Store page describe what it currently does.

(2) You use The Alarm without an account. What you enter lives on your iPhone; The Alarm doesn't need a server of ours for this.

(3) We keep developing The Alarm. We may change The Alarm if there is a valid reason – such as new technology, security, a change in the law, changes to Apple services The Alarm uses, or new features –, if this causes you no additional costs and if we inform you clearly about the change. If a change affects your access to The Alarm Pro or its usability more than insignificantly, we inform you in good time beforehand about the nature and timing of the change and about your right to end the contract free of charge within 30 days of this information or, if later, of the change (Section 327r of the German Civil Code, BGB).

3. Your data and backups

(1) The Alarm keeps everything on your iPhone. We receive none of it – and therefore can't restore anything either. If you lose your iPhone or delete the app, your data is gone unless you have a backup.

(2) So set up a backup under Settings › Backup, in a place you choose. If you set a password, nobody can open the backup without it, not even us. Keep it in a safe place.

4. The Alarm Pro

(1) The Alarm is free, without ads and without a limit on the number of alarms. What reliable waking needs – including the evening check, the safety net and the trial run – stays free. The Alarm Pro unlocks more, such as shift plans, important alarms, the wake-up check, your own and talking sounds, the good-morning card, the morning routine, alarms by your calendar and by the sun, and the designs. The app shows what's included before you buy.

(2) The Alarm Pro is available as a subscription – monthly or yearly – and as a one-time purchase. You buy it through the App Store; Apple handles the payment, and Apple's Media Services Terms and Conditions apply. You see the prices in the app before you buy, including VAT.

(3) Subscriptions renew automatically for the chosen period unless you cancel at least 24 hours before it ends. Payment is charged to your Apple Account when you confirm the purchase, and for each renewal within 24 hours before the end of the period. After you cancel, the subscription stays active until the end of the paid period. You manage and cancel subscriptions in your Apple Account settings.

(4) If you haven't had a subscription to The Alarm Pro before, you can try the yearly subscription free for 14 days. Unless you cancel at least 24 hours before the end of the free trial, the paid subscription begins. The monthly subscription and the one-time purchase have no free trial.

(5) You pay for the one-time purchase once; it doesn't renew. If you also have a subscription, it keeps running until you cancel it with Apple.

(6) You can share The Alarm Pro with up to five other family members through Apple's Family Sharing – both the subscription and the one-time purchase.

(7) If you switch between the monthly and the yearly subscription, Apple governs when the new subscription applies and how the current period is credited.

(8) When Pro ends, your alarms keep ringing the way you set them up – including everything you set up with Pro. Your data is kept. Without Pro, you just can't change Pro settings or turn them on again. Designs, colors, fonts and app icons from Pro go back to the default; with Pro, your settings come back.

(9) Because Apple handles the purchase, refunds and the right of withdrawal are governed by Apple's rules. You can request a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com. Your statutory rights remain unaffected.

5. Your content

(1) What you add to The Alarm belongs to you. It lives on your iPhone; we don't receive it.

(2) Only import sound files you're allowed to use, and only record other people's voices with their consent.

6. Sharing

(1) You share a shift plan without a server: as a QR code or as a file, through apps you choose. It only contains the plan's name, pattern, start and kinds of shifts. We don't see shared plans and don't store them.

(2) Only share a plan if you're allowed to pass it on. If you open a plan someone sent you, check the alarm times before you save – everyone needs different ones. You save a new shift plan, including a shared one, with Pro.

(3) The same applies to a single alarm you share as a QR code or as a file (“.thealarm”) and to a running timer or a stopwatch you share as a QR code or a link: no server of ours is involved, and we don't see or store them. Only share what you're allowed to pass on; The Alarm only takes over what someone sends you once you confirm it.

7. What The Alarm reads, calculates and suggests

(1) The Alarm reads rosters automatically – on iPhones with Apple Intelligence also with its help, on the device. Apple Intelligence also fills in alarms in your own words. This can be wrong: check every day and every setting before you save. More in the AI notice.

(2) The Alarm works out the snooze coach, bedtime, the sleep plan and the sleep shown in the week view from your alarms, shifts and mornings. These are suggestions, not medical advice.

(3) The shift count and rest times count what's in your plan. What gets paid and which rest times apply to you is governed by law, collective agreements and your employer. The Alarm is no replacement for legal advice or payroll.

(4) The Alarm comes with public holidays for the years 2026 to 2028, from the free date-holidays collection. They may be incomplete or change, especially regional holidays. Check under Settings › Public Holidays whether they're right for you. For later years, The Alarm needs an update.

(5) Weather forecasts come from Apple and can be wrong. The weather lead only asks you whether your alarm should ring earlier; it doesn't change anything by itself.

(6) Alarms by your calendar follow the events The Alarm finds in your calendar. If you add an early event at short notice, briefly open The Alarm afterwards so it can reschedule the alarm.

8. Reliability of alarms

(1) The Alarm sets your alarms through AlarmKit, the alarm system of iOS. In the end, iOS decides when an alarm rings. We do a lot to make your alarms ring on time, but we can't guarantee it. An alarm may ring late or not at all – for example because of a bug in iOS (there are reports that iOS sometimes fires alarms late on locked iPhones at night), if your iPhone is off or its battery is empty, if you take back the permission for alarms, or if your iPhone's date, time or time zone are wrong.

(2) For appointments you mustn't miss under any circumstances – a flight, an exam, the start of a shift –, don't rely on a single alarm: turn on the safety net (a notification at the same time as the alarm), use the trial run to check that you hear your alarm, use a backup alarm with Pro, and set a second alarm as well, ideally on another device.

(3) The Alarm schedules alarms with rules for the next 30 days at a time and keeps rescheduling them: when you open the app, after every change and in the background, as far as iOS allows. So open The Alarm every now and then; the evening check tells you whether everything is scheduled.

(4) We can't promise that the Apple services The Alarm uses – such as weather, Home or Apple Intelligence – are always available. If one fails, only the feature that needs it is missing; your alarms still ring.

(5) Your statutory rights in case of defects of digital products (Sections 327 et seq. BGB) remain unaffected. We provide the updates needed to keep The Alarm in line with the contract (Section 327f BGB). Install updates to use The Alarm securely and reliably.
